# feedback04 — ring signal volume, empty alerts, temporal ranking

Four defects reported against the real extract, one framing problem on the case page, and one layout request. The largest defect and the framing problem turned out to be the same story: a ring is about a **card**, and the system was treating both the card's identifier and the card's case as if they belonged to a merchant.

---

## 1. Ring signal — 65,000+ alerts, almost none of them true

**Reported:** *"there were 65k+ alerts from ring signal indicating something is wrong with the way we are measuring this and I suspect that its due to the fact that maybe the calculation are trying to capture wallets but instead it should only look at the banks credit cards or debit cards."*

The suspicion was exactly right, and the mechanism was narrower than a scoping mistake.

### What happened

Wallet rails have no card number to hash. The source does not omit the column — **it writes `""`**. An empty string is not NULL, so every wallet row passed the `hashed_pan IS NOT NULL` filter and then compared **equal to every other wallet row**. Fifty-two percent of the portfolio grouped as one card.

Measured on the busiest day of the real extract:

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Rows in the largest "card" chain | 79,891 | — (blank is not a card) |
| Merchants that chain touched | **2,592** | — |
| Largest *real* card fanout | 4 merchants | 4 merchants |
| Real cards reaching the 5-merchant swarm threshold | 0 | **0** |
| Genuine impossible-travel findings | buried | **5 legs, 5 alerts** |

The 65,000 alerts were the artefact. The layer's true volume on this data is single digits.

### The fix — three places, deliberately redundant

The failure was silent and produced confident output, so it is guarded more than once:

1. **`ingest._text` folds blank to NULL**, on both the JSON and CSV load paths. "Absent" now behaves like absent. This also fixes the identity side: merchants with a blank `hashed_br_number` would otherwise all join one enormous ring.
2. **The ring query rejects `""` explicitly**, covering rows loaded before (1).
3. **`rings.WALLET_RAILS` excludes Alipay, Octopus, WeChat, PayMe, FPS and Tap&Go by name.** The blank check alone would start producing rings again the day the source begins emitting a per-wallet token — and one Alipay account used at many shops is a customer, not a ring.

Regression cover: the synthetic generator now includes six wallet-only merchants, spread as far apart as the geo-velocity pair and trading at the same times, all carrying `hashed_pan=""`. No card-linkage rule may raise an alert on any of them.

## 2. One card, one alert

**Reported:** *"Each hashed pan that is having suspicious transactions should be all placed under the same case with one singular alert raised to keep the number of alerts down to sensible number."*

Every card-linkage rule attributed its finding to **each participating merchant**, and geo-velocity did it per leg *and* per end of that leg. One card ping-ponging between two districts for an afternoon produced a dozen alerts describing one story.

All three rules now report **once per card**. Attribution is deterministic and lands where the question is:

| Rule | Carries the alert |
|---|---|
| One card across related merchants | the branch with the most of that card's transactions that day |
| Card swarming | the merchant with the most of that card's transactions in the window |
| Impossible geo-velocity | the **arrival** end of the fastest leg — the merchant that accepted a card which could not have been present |

Nothing is lost: the other merchants are named in the finding, listed in `linkage.merchants`, and every one of their transactions is in the evidence.

On the synthetic fixture, `card_across_related_merchants` went 4 alerts → 1 and `impossible_geo_velocity` 2 → 1.

## 3. The case page is about the card, not about a merchant

**Reported:** *"the information in that case review page will not be of a specific singular merchant it will be of the hashed pan that we are tracking and of all merchants it transacted to... we will have one alert because of one card and address all transactions and times and merchants and locations and whatever other information that would be necessary all in own clear page."*

Collapsing to one alert (§2) was only half of it. The page was still *framed* as a merchant: it opened with the merchant identity band — name, ID, MCC, location, business nature — and one of the ten merchants the card visited happened to be that frame. The other nine were a tab away.

For a card-linkage alert the merchant band is now replaced by a **card band**:

| | |
|---|---|
| Card | `card 2 · identifier withheld` |
| Merchants touched · Districts | every one, named |
| Window · Transactions · Total value · Rails | the whole trail |
| Common ownership | branches of one chain, or unrelated |

…followed immediately by the merchants themselves — trade, location, when seen, count and value each — with the attributed one marked *alert filed here*. It is one row among the others rather than the subject of the page.

The page **opens on the Card trail**. The merchant's own day is demoted to the last tab and renamed *This merchant's day*, because on a card case that is context, not evidence.

The trail was also widened: it now shows **the card's whole day**, not only the transactions that tripped the rule. A finding showing two transactions 20 minutes and 35 km apart immediately raises "what else did this card do?", and the answer belongs on the page. It is assembled at detection time inside `rings.py` — which is what keeps `hashed_pan` in the detection layer, since the case page still only resolves transaction ids. The triggering transactions stay marked, so widening the evidence does not blur what the accusation rests on, and the arrival end of an impossible leg carries its implied speed on its own row.

## 4. The evidence a linked-card finding actually needs

**Reported:** *"we should rather in transaction list show all transactions from this same hashed pan in the different merchants and highlight the times... In the statistical reasoning we can view the calculations of the distance between the different districts and time delta and speed calculations and that its higher than the threshold set by how much."*

This was the real gap. A ring finding is the one case where the merchant's own day ledger **cannot** hold the evidence — half of it happened at somebody else's shop — so an analyst either took the verdict on trust or opened each counterparty by hand.

**New:** `GET /api/alerts/{id}/linked-transactions`, and a **Card trail** tab that appears only on alerts that have one.

- The card's transactions across every merchant it touched, in time order.
- **Gap to the previous transaction as a column** — that gap is what the impossible-travel claim rests on, so it is a column rather than something to work out. Rows the rule actually fired on are marked; the rest of the card's day is shown around them, dimmed.
- A **Travel** column carrying the implied speed on the row it condemns, so the arithmetic is not only in a separate table further down.
- Place, MCC and amount per row; the merchant carrying the alert marked; a **Chain** label wherever merchants share a registration, address or trading name. That last one answers *"if we are looking at transactions... to different chains of the same store then we should highlight it"* directly.

**Under Statistical proof**, impossible travel now shows its arithmetic leg by leg: the two places, centroid distance, elapsed minutes, implied km/h, the limit, and the multiple over it. With the note that centroid distance under-states the real journey, so the test under-flags by construction.

The card itself never crosses the boundary. `card_ref` ("card 1", "card 2") is a **position in the run's sort order** — not a truncation or re-hash of the PAN hash, since either of those is still a function of cardholder data. Enforced by test.

## 5. Empty MCC peer discrepancy alerts

**Reported:** *"all the limited history merchants have this type of alert which doesn't make sense. like i click and then shows 0 alerts fired then why is it there?"* — with a screenshot of merchant 10068: `0 Fired · 2 Passed · 10 Skipped`, reason *"Skipped (MCC peer merchant baseline not usable)"*, median 16,280 from N=2 against an MCC median of 324, zero transactions on the scored day.

The pipeline and the case page each held **their own copy** of the gate, and they had drifted:

- Pipeline fired on `peer_usable AND baseline_center`.
- Case page additionally required `baseline_usable`, and reported SKIP without it.

A merchant with two transactions in its whole window has a `baseline_center` — the median of two numbers is always defined — and no baseline. So the alert fired and its own evidence page denied it.

**Fixed** with a single predicate, `merchant_study.merchant_level_is_comparable`, consulted by both sides. It returns the *reason* rather than a boolean, so the sentence the analyst reads is produced by the same call that decided not to fire. Three conditions:

1. A usable MCC peer cohort.
2. A usable baseline of the merchant's own.
3. **Trade on the scored day.** Added on top of the reported defect: the baseline window is lagged and does not move overnight, so a silent merchant produced the identical alert every night until somebody dispositioned it. That is not a daily signal, it is the same alert re-queued — and it is why the queue filled with merchants showing zero transactions.

`tests/test_alert_is_explainable.py` now asserts the invariant rather than either copy of the condition: **no alert may open to zero fired**, whatever raised it.

## 6. Temporal anomaly ranking

**Reported:** *"The way that the rank of temporary anomaly is calculated is broken, because all of them are ranked 0.5... Maybe use the volume of temporarily abnormal transactions to judge how severe the alert is."*

Both timing detectors hard-coded `sub_score = 0.5`. Rank decides which case is opened first, so every temporal alert was tied and ordered by the tie-break.

`timedensity.temporal_severity` now scores three inputs, bounded to [0, 1] so it stays comparable with the z-score detectors it is ranked against:

| Input | Weight | What it separates |
|---|---|---|
| Count of off-hours transactions (log-saturating at 100) | 0.55 | a stray late sale from a night's trading |
| Their share of the day | 0.30 | a merchant operating at the wrong time from a late closer |
| How far below the merchant's own cutoff the worst sat | 0.15 | an hour never traded from the quiet edge of the evening |

Volume carries the most, as requested. Depth is measured against the merchant's *own* threshold, because densities are not comparable between merchants — a round-the-clock forecourt's busiest hour is thinner than a boutique's quietest.

On the synthetic fixture, temporal scores now span **0.108 → 0.730** where all were 0.500.

## 7. Per-MCC override placement

**Reported:** *"Place the MCC specific outlier toggle directly under the global one because it is the only one that can be overridden in the tuning tab."*

Done. It was a card of its own further down the page, which implied the other thresholds could be overridden too and put the exception a screen away from the rule it excepts. It now sits inside the threshold list, directly beneath the outlier slider, and says in one line that it is the only overridable one.

## Verification

- **309 backend tests pass** (241 before; 68 added).
- Frontend builds clean; lint unchanged.
- Both ring cases walked in the browser against a seeded run. The geo-velocity case opens on the card band — *card 2*, 2 merchants, Tung Chung · Sai Kung, 14:00–14:20, HKD 1,040, VISA, unrelated — then the 35.5 km / 20 min / 106 km/h / 1.8× calculation. The branch case shows *card 1* across 4 merchants, 12:00–13:15, HKD 7,200, all labelled **Chain A**, with **Yes — branches of one chain**. Both default to the Card trail tab, with the single-merchant day last.
- Tuning verified end to end — adding an MCC exception in its new position persists.
- Real-extract measurements in §1 computed directly from `real_data/`, not from the synthetic fixture.

## Not done

- **Case grouping across families.** The report noted that for Family B and C, one merchant's five alerts collapsing on one disposition "might simply not be the best way of doing it", while confirming the current workflow is acceptable. The card-linkage rules now raise one alert per card, which removes the pressure there. A broader change to case grouping was left alone deliberately — it changes the audit trail's unit of decision, and that is worth deciding explicitly rather than as a side effect of a bug fix.
